Reporting that your model beat the state of the art is easy. Reporting it in a
way that survives someone reproducing you is a different job, and it is mostly
done before you train anything.

## The number is the easy part

PLACEHOLDER — open with your own figure. Dice 0.7984 against a U-Net
ResNet-34 baseline is a sentence anyone can write; what makes it meaningful is
everything in the next three sections.

## Same split, or you did not race

The most common way to beat a baseline is to accidentally give yourself an
easier test set. Fix the split first, freeze it, and run both models through
the identical preprocessing — including the parts you think are neutral.

## Report the metric that can hurt you

PLACEHOLDER — precision and recall separately, not just the aggregate. An
aggregate score hides which kind of mistake your model prefers, and in a
diagnostic setting that preference is the whole story.

## Say where it breaks

PLACEHOLDER — the section most papers skip. A margin that vanishes on a
different split is worth knowing about, and stating it yourself is cheaper than
having a reviewer find it.
