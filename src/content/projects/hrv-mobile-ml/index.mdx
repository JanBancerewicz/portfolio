Heart rate variability is a genuinely useful signal — it tracks stress,
recovery and autonomic state — and measuring it properly normally means a chest
strap. This work asks how much of that you can recover from a phone camera
pressed against a fingertip, and answers it with a model rather than with
better optics.

## The problem

Photoplethysmographic signals from a phone are noisy in every way that matters:
the hand moves, the exposure drifts, ambient light leaks in, and the sampling
rate is whatever the camera feels like giving you. Classical peak detection
falls apart well before the signal becomes unusable to a human eye.

## The approach

A CNN front end learns the morphology of a pulse waveform under exactly those
distortions; an LSTM stage carries the temporal structure that HRV is actually
made of. The pipeline is deterministic end to end — every published number
regenerates from the raw recordings with one command.

## Result

Published as **Analysis of HRV Using Mobile Devices and Machine Learning** in
TASK Quarterly (2026). In July 2026 the work was accepted into the
**Europe AI Summer Research programme**.

## What is next

I am turning it into a production-ready mobile app rather than leaving it as a
research artefact — which means the boring half of the work starts now: battery
cost, capture guidance for people who have never held their finger on a camera
for ninety seconds, and honest error bars when the signal is simply too poor to
report a number.
